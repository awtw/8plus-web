# Google will support a shared AI training control

On October 30, the Google Search Central blog published a short post: [Supporting a shared AI training control for websites](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/10/ai-training-control), by John Mueller. The point is simple: **Google plans to support one robots.txt training control shared across AI providers.**

For personal sites, blogs and portfolios, this is one of the few search announcements this year that actually affects you.

## 1. What it is

- **Mechanism:** a shared "policy token" in robots.txt that says "do not use this site's content to train AI".
- **Origin:** the IETF AIPREF working group ([draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/)). It is not a Google-only spec.
- **Syntax:** Google's [crawler documentation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers#google-extended) currently refers to it as `user-agent: __usage_ai-training`. The spec is still a draft, so the final syntax may change.
- **Timing:** Google says it aims to support it alongside other AI providers in **2027**; the documentation says early 2027.

## 2. How it relates to Google-Extended

Google already has its own control, `Google-Extended`, which governs whether content is used for Gemini training and grounding. The shared control does not replace it:

- If a site opts out with the shared token **and does not mention Google-Extended**, Google will treat it as if Google-Extended were disallowed.
- If a site **explicitly sets Google-Extended**, that rule wins.
- Each company's existing controls keep working.

Google's documentation is also clear that **Google-Extended does not affect whether a site appears in Google Search and is not a ranking signal.** So the worry that opting out of training hurts your ranking does not hold, at least on the Google-Extended side.

## 3. What it does not cover

This is the part most likely to be misread:

- It controls **training**, not search. Whether you are indexed is still up to `Googlebot`.
- The announcement does not mention AI Overviews or AI Mode. If you want to stay out of AI summaries entirely, this token is not the answer.
- Conversely, Google notes that **sites that explicitly allow Google-Extended are eligible to appear in Gemini Apps responses and receive clicks via grounding.**

So there is a trade-off: opting out of training and grounding also means giving up being cited in Gemini answers.

## 4. What site owners can do now

### 1. Decide where you stand

Ask yourself: do I want AI to learn from my content and cite it?

- **Freelancers, consultants, personal brands:** you usually want to be seen and cited. Stay open and put the effort into content quality.
- **Paid content, original work, photography and illustration:** you may care more about training use and choose to opt out.

### 2. What works today: Google-Extended

If you want to stop Gemini from using your content, you can add this to robots.txt now:

```txt
User-agent: Google-Extended
Disallow: /
```

You can also block only some paths. Google's own example allows a single page and blocks the rest of a directory.

### 3. Add the shared token once the spec is final

`__usage_ai-training` is still an IETF draft, and Google expects to support it in 2027. Note it now and add it when it is officially live, so you are not shipping a rule nobody reads.

### 4. On Next.js, robots lives in code

This site uses `app/robots.ts`. The current rules allow everything except the admin and tool pages. My position is **open**: these articles are meant to be read and cited, and the site also serves `/llms.txt` so AI tools can understand its structure more easily.
